# Internet Banking User Satisfaction Analysis

### Master's-Level Data Analytics Case Study

**Tools:** SPSS | R | PCA | Exploratory Factor Analysis | Multiple Linear Regression | Statistical Analysis

---

## Project Overview

This project investigates the factors affecting users' satisfaction with internet banking services.

The study was developed from Master's-level research in Statistics and used primary survey data collected from internet banking users among staff at Monywa University of Economics and Monywa University.

The objective was to identify the underlying dimensions of internet banking service perception and determine which factors had the strongest relationship with user satisfaction.

---

## Research Questions

1. What factors influence users' satisfaction with internet banking services?
2. Which perceived internet banking service factors significantly affect user satisfaction?

---

## Dataset

- Primary survey data
- 316 respondents
- Stratified random sampling
- 7-point Likert scale
- Cross-sectional study design
- Survey period: July 2020

Due to time constraints, the study was limited to staff from two universities in Monywa (Sagaing Region).

---

## Analytical Workflow

The project followed an end-to-end analytical process:

1. Survey design and primary data collection
2. Descriptive statistical analysis
3. Reliability testing using Cronbach's Alpha
4. Exploratory Factor Analysis
5. Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
6. Varimax rotation
7. Correlation analysis
8. Multiple Linear Regression
9. ANOVA
10. Multicollinearity and residual diagnostics
11. Business interpretation and recommendations

---

## PCA & Factor Analysis

The analysis reduced the retained variables into six underlying factors:

1. Responsiveness
2. Perceived Usefulness
3. Trust & Security
4. Better Services
5. Perceived Ease of Use
6. Effectiveness

### Factor Analysis Diagnostics

- KMO = 0.90
- Bartlett's Test: p < 0.001
- Cronbach's Alpha for internet banking service items = 0.91
- Six factors explained approximately 49.5% of cumulative variance

---

## Regression Results

The final multiple regression model achieved:

- R = 0.774
- R² = 0.599
- Adjusted R² = 0.594
- F = 116.158
- p < 0.001

This means that approximately **59.4% of the variation in user satisfaction** was explained by the selected factors in the final model.

### Significant Predictors

| Predictor | Standardized Beta | p-value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Better Services | 0.470 | < 0.001 |
| Perceived Ease of Use | 0.291 | < 0.001 |
| Effectiveness | 0.116 | 0.009 |
| Responsiveness | 0.082 | 0.047 |

**Better Services** emerged as the strongest significant predictor of user satisfaction.

---

## Key Business Insights

### 1. Service improvement is the primary driver

Better Services had the strongest standardized effect on satisfaction.

This suggests that banks should prioritize:

- Additional digital services
- After-sale support
- App maintenance and upgrades
- Improved online-service delivery

### 2. Ease of use is critical

Perceived Ease of Use was the second strongest significant predictor.

Banks should focus on:

- Simplifying transaction procedures
- Improving online payment flows
- Reducing unnecessary steps
- Making digital banking easier to navigate

### 3. Effectiveness and responsiveness still matter

Customers also value:

- Efficient transactions
- Timely support
- Knowledgeable staff
- Responsive problem resolution

### 4. Correlation does not equal independent impact

Perceived Usefulness and Trust & Security were positively correlated with user satisfaction but were not statistically significant predictors in the final regression model.

Therefore, they should not be interpreted as independent drivers of satisfaction in this sample.

---

## Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, banks should:

- Prioritize service quality improvements
- Improve after-sale and customer support
- Simplify digital transaction procedures
- Improve online payment experience
- Maintain and upgrade banking applications
- Improve responsiveness to customer issues
- Continue strengthening user confidence in digital banking services
